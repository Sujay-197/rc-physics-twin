# Campus shuttle: an autonomous bus game on the twin

Status: design, 2026-10-21. Builds on the campus player (`build/campus/RcTwinCampus.exe`) and its autopilot (`TwinAutopilot`).

## Intent

The twin can now drive itself between any two campus stops reliably: pure pursuit tuned for the Unity car, the corner speed plan, the kerb recovery and the driven turn-round finish every evaluation trip at 1 to 4 m/s (`Docs/policy-study.md`). This turns that into something people want to watch: a campus shuttle that runs itself.

Students raise ride requests at stops, each from one stop to another. The game generates them, and the person watching can add more. One shuttle decides on its own whom to serve and in what order, picks students up, carries several at once, drops them off, and keeps an eye on its battery, going to charge at AB1 when it must. A score shows how well it is doing. It runs as an endless sandbox, and it should look like a believable campus shuttle on the real campus scenery already in the scene.

## The first minute

The shuttle waits at a stop. A student appears at AB2 with a label "→ S6" and a wait timer. The shuttle decides to take it: it turns round if the route leaves the other way, drives to AB2, stops, the student boards, and it drives to S6, where the student gets off. Meanwhile more students appear at other stops; the shuttle picks some up on the way when it has seats. The battery gauge drains as it drives. When it runs low, the shuttle stops taking new requests, finishes or postpones what it carries as its battery allows, and drives to AB1 to charge. The score climbs with each delivery.

## Decided with the user

- **Fully autonomous.** The person watching never drives or dispatches. They watch and can raise requests; the shuttle does the rest.
- **Requests**: generated by the game, and the user can add one (pick a stop, then a destination).
- **One shuttle now, several later.** The design keeps deciding *which shuttle serves a request* apart from *how one shuttle plans its stops*, so more shuttles is an addition, not a rewrite.
- **Pooling with a capacity.** The shuttle carries several students at once, up to a seat count, and may pick up while others are on board.
- **Charging at AB1.** When the battery gets low the shuttle stops accepting new passengers and gets to AB1. Whether it first drops off the students on board or goes to charge with them aboard, it decides itself, from the energy it has.
- **A score.**
- **Screen layout C**: the 3D campus fills the screen; a minimap top-right (campus roads, stops with waiting counts, the shuttle, AB1's charger); a bottom bar with battery, seats, the shuttle's next stop and the request queue; the score top-left. Labels over each waiting student give their destination.
- **Look: realistic-ish.** A believable white campus shuttle with shading and shadows, plain human figures, understated labels, matching the real campus scenery.
- **Endless sandbox**, no rounds or end screen.

## Goals

1. Requests appear at stops by the stops' demand weights (`campus/campus_network.json`: S3 6, AB4 4, the rest 1), and the user can add one.
2. The shuttle serves them autonomously with sensible pooling, within its capacity, and never strands itself on purpose.
3. The battery matters within minutes of watching, and the shuttle's charging decisions are visible and sensible.
4. A score that rewards quick, direct service.
5. It looks like a campus shuttle game, not a physics test bench.

## Non-goals

- Changing the car's physics, its parameters or the autopilot's driving. Default SlipX parameters are the car (there is no real car to measure). The shuttle is the twin's car with a shuttle body on it.
- Manual driving or manual dispatch as game modes. The existing keyboard driving may stay as a debug fallback, but nothing in the game relies on it.
- Several shuttles in this version (only the seam for them).
- Learned policies in the game. The autopilot's pure pursuit drives; the learned-driver toggle can stay but is not part of the game.
- Networking, saving, menus beyond a minimal settings panel.

## Anti-goals: it fails if

- The shuttle ever looks dumb: driving past a waiting student with free seats when they are on its way, dithering between plans, or running flat because it planned badly.
- The battery never matters (the real pack lasts hours) or empties so fast that the shuttle does nothing but charge.
- The new look breaks the driving: the body changes the car's footprint, walls or physics.
- Students teleport into the shuttle with no visible boarding moment.

## How it works

### Requests

- Each stop spawns students as a Poisson process, rate proportional to its demand weight. Destination: another stop drawn by demand weight, never the same node (as `CampusNetwork.sample_trip` draws trips). *My call:* about one new request every 20 s across the campus at the start, adjustable in settings.
- The user raises a request by clicking a stop (in the 3D view or on the minimap), then choosing a destination from the stop list.
- A request is: origin, destination, time raised, number of students (1 for now).
- A student waits at most 3 minutes (*my call*), then gives up and leaves.

### The shuttle's plan

- **Capacity:** 6 seats (*my call*, a setting).
- **The plan** is an ordered list of stops to visit, each with who boards and who alights there. Every accepted request has its pickup before its drop-off, and the load never exceeds capacity along the plan.
- **Accepting requests** (*my call*: cheapest insertion). When a request appears, the shuttle tries every place to insert its pickup and drop-off into the current plan and keeps the cheapest that respects capacity and the battery rule below. Cost: the added driving time plus the extra waiting and riding time it causes the students already in the plan. Travel times between stops come from route lengths (`CampusRoutes.Route`) at the cruise speed, plus a fixed dwell at each stop and a turn-round allowance when a leg leaves a stop the way the shuttle came in. If no insertion is allowed (full, or not enough battery), the request waits unassigned and is retried whenever the plan changes.
- **Commitment:** the next stop in the plan is fixed once the shuttle has left for it, so it never U-turns mid-road to chase a new request. Later stops may be reordered.
- **Stopping on the way** (*my call*): stops sit on the through roads (62 of the 90 routes pass at least one other stop), so a student often waits at a stop the shuttle is about to drive through. Like a bus, the shuttle stops there when someone is waiting with a seat free for them, or someone aboard gets off there; otherwise it drives through. This needs one addition to `TwinAutopilot`: end the current trip early at a stop on the route ahead (`StopEarlyAt`), when there is room to brake for it at the stop plan's 1.2 m/s². The driving itself (pure pursuit, corner plan, kerb recovery) is unchanged; the trip's road and corner plan are cut at that stop.
- **Driving:** each leg of the plan is one `TwinAutopilot.Go(next stop)`. The autopilot drives it as today (turn-round, corner speed plan, kerb recovery), at the cruise speed (default 2 m/s, the panel's default).
- **At a stop:** the shuttle stops (the autopilot's arrival), the alighting students get off, then the boarding ones get on, a few seconds each, visibly (they walk to and from the door). Then it leaves for the next stop.
- **Idle:** with nothing to do, the shuttle waits at the stop it is at. *My call:* if the battery is below 60 % and idle, it goes to top up at AB1.
- **The seam for several shuttles:** a dispatcher receives each request and asks every shuttle for its insertion cost; with one shuttle it simply gives it to that one. Each shuttle owns its plan.

### Battery

- **A game battery.** The real pack would last about 2,500 cross-campus trips. The shuttle gets a smaller pack capacity so that a full charge lasts about 5 minutes of driving at the cruise speed (*my call*, a setting). It changes nothing else: same car, same motor, same voltage curve, so a low battery also means a little less power, as on a real pack.
- **Energy estimate:** energy per metre measured as the shuttle drives (a running average of its own consumption), seeded from the twin's measured value. The energy for any leg is its route length times that, times 1.15 for safety (*my call*).
- **The rule:** the shuttle never accepts a plan whose energy, plus the energy to reach AB1 from its last stop, exceeds the energy left minus a 10 % reserve (*my call*). So a low battery shows up naturally as requests it cannot take.
- **Low battery:** when the energy left minus the reserve no longer covers the current plan plus getting to AB1, the shuttle stops accepting requests and works out the best of these two:
  1. drop off everyone on board (pickups of those not yet on board are released back to waiting), then go to AB1;
  2. go to AB1 now with them aboard, charge, then deliver them.

  It takes 1 if it has the energy for it, else 2. The bottom bar says so ("Battery low: dropping off 2, then charging" / "Battery low: charging first").
- **Charging at AB1:** a charger sits at the AB1 stop. Parked there, the battery fills at a rate that takes it from 10 to 95 % in about 30 s (*my call*, a setting), shown on the gauge and with a charging effect. Students on board stay aboard; students waiting at AB1 may board while it charges. It leaves at 95 %.
- **Flat battery:** the Core has no cutoff (charge stops at 0 % and the car still drives), so flat is a game rule: at 0.5 % charge the shuttle stops, −500 points, and after a few seconds it is towed: it reappears at AB1 and charges. Students on board are delivered by it afterwards.
- **A failed trip** (*my call*): if the autopilot gives up on a trip (left the route, ran past the stop, timed out) or cannot start one, the shuttle is towed to the stop it was driving to and carries on; students aboard stay aboard; no penalty beyond kerb strikes. The autopilot's existing fallback when no turn-round can be planned (turning the car at the stop, "not driven") is accepted.

### Score

*My call, all values in settings:*

- Each delivered student: +100, minus 1 per second they waited at the stop (at least +20).
- +20 more if their ride took no more than 1.5 times the direct trip.
- A student who gives up: −50.
- A flat battery: −500.
- Each kerb strike: −5, counted once per scrape (the car's wall counter counts every physics frame in contact).
- Shown with the score: delivered, average wait, gave up.

### Screen (layout C)

- **Top-left:** score, then delivered · average wait · gave up.
- **Top-right, minimap:** the campus roads from `campus_network.json`, stops as dots with the number waiting, the shuttle as a marker with its heading, its planned path, AB1 with a charger icon. Clicking a stop on it starts a request.
- **Bottom bar:** battery gauge with percent and charging state, seats used/total, what the shuttle is doing ("To AB2: pick up 2", "Charging at AB1, 74 %"), then the request queue as chips (origin → destination, wait time, assigned or waiting).
- **In the world:** a small label over each waiting student or group with their destination and wait; a subtle seat count over the shuttle.
- **Settings** (small panel, closed by default): request rate, capacity, cruise speed, charge duration, battery duration, pause, and "add request".
- The existing debug panels (`TwinCar`'s status, `TwinAutopilot`'s panel) are hidden in the game and can be toggled with a key for debugging.

### Look (realistic-ish)

- **Shuttle:** a white campus shuttle body (with a coloured stripe, windows, a door on the kerb side) over the twin's car, at the car's 1/10 scale. Purely visual: the car's footprint, walls, contact and physics are unchanged. The wheels keep turning as today.
- **Students:** simple human figures at 1/10 scale, varied clothing colours, idle animation while waiting, walking to and from the door.
- **Stops:** a shelter and a sign at each stop; at AB1, a charging post.
- Shadows and lighting as the campus scene has; no change to the scenery.

## Constraints

- Unity 6000.5.3f1, the existing RcTwin project and campus scene; the Core and `rc_twin` plugin unchanged (ABI 7).
- The autopilot and its driving stay as they are. The game sits on top of `TwinAutopilot` (one stop-to-stop trip at a time) and `CampusRoutes`.
- The campus is 1/10 scale: speeds and distances are the twin's (a 2 m/s cruise is about 72 km/h at full scale; that is the twin's world and stays).
- The campus player build (`CampusSceneBuilder.BuildPlayerBatch`) produces the game. *My call:* one scene; the player starts in game mode, and `-goto <stop>` on the command line still runs the one-trip autopilot demo instead. In the editor the game stays off unless a test or the inspector turns it on, so the existing Play Mode tests that drive `TwinAutopilot` directly are unaffected.
- Testable logic (requests, plan insertion, battery rule, score) lives in plain C# free of `UnityEngine`, like `Drivers.cs`, with Edit Mode tests; a Play Mode test runs the game headless for a few simulated minutes and checks it delivers students and never runs flat.

## Left to the builder

- Code structure, names, and how the game objects are organised in the scene.
- Assets: making the shuttle body, figures and shelters from primitives and simple meshes, or importing free assets, as long as the look is realistic-ish and licences are recorded in `THIRD_PARTY.md`.
- UI technology (the existing immediate-mode GUI or Unity's UI Toolkit/uGUI) as long as the layout is C.
- Exact visuals of the minimap, labels and effects.
- Time acceleration (a fast-forward button) if cheap.
